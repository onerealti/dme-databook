# Cotter and Knuckle Joints — Worked Examples

---

### Example 12.1

Design and draw a cotter joint to support a load varying from $30\text{ kN}$ in compression to $30\text{ kN}$ in tension. The material used is carbon steel for which the following allowable stresses may be used. The load is applied statically.

- **Tensile stress** ($\sigma_t$) = **Compressive stress** ($\sigma_c$) = $50\text{ MPa}$
- **Shear stress** ($\tau$) = $35\text{ MPa}$
- **Crushing stress** ($\sigma_c$) = $90\text{ MPa}$

---

### Example 12.2

Design a sleeve and cotter joint to resist a tensile load of $60\text{ kN}$. All parts of the joint are made of the same material with the following allowable stresses:

- $\sigma_t = 60\text{ MPa}$
- $\tau = 70\text{ MPa}$
- $\sigma_c = 125\text{ MPa}$

---

### Example 12.3

The big end of a connecting rod is subjected to a maximum load of $50\text{ kN}$. The diameter of the circular part of the rod adjacent to the strap end is $75\text{ mm}$. Design the gib and cotter joint, assuming:
- Permissible tensile stress for the strap material ($\sigma_t$) = $25\text{ MPa}$
- Permissible shear stress for the cotter and gib material ($\tau$) = $20\text{ MPa}$

---

### Example 12.7

Design a knuckle joint to transmit $150\text{ kN}$. The design stresses may be taken as:

- **Tension** ($\sigma_t$) = $75\text{ MPa}$
- **Shear** ($\tau$) = $60\text{ MPa}$
- **Compression / Crushing** ($\sigma_c$) = $150\text{ MPa}$

---

### Example 12.8

Design a knuckle joint for a tie rod of a circular section to sustain a maximum pull of $70\text{ kN}$. 
- Ultimate strength of the rod material against tearing = $420\text{ MPa}$
- Ultimate tensile strength of the pin material = $510\text{ MPa}$
- Ultimate shearing strength of the pin material = $396\text{ MPa}$
- **Factor of Safety (FOS)** = $6$

Determine the tie rod section and pin section.
