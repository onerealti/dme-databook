# Welded Joints — Solved Examples

### Example 10.1 (Double Parallel Fillet Weld)
A plate $100\text{ mm}$ wide and $10\text{ mm}$ thick is to be welded to another plate by means of double parallel fillets. The plates are subjected to a static load of $80\text{ kN}$. Find the length of weld if the permissible shear stress in the weld does not exceed $55\text{ MPa}$.

---

### Example 10.2 (Circular Fillet Weld under Torsion)
A $50\text{ mm}$ diameter solid shaft is welded to a flat plate by a $10\text{ mm}$ fillet weld. Find the maximum torque that the welded joint can sustain if the maximum shear stress intensity in the weld material is not to exceed $80\text{ MPa}$.

---

### Example 10.3 (Rectangular Plate Fillet Weld under Torsion)
A plate $1\text{ m}$ long, $60\text{ mm}$ thick is welded to another plate at right angles to each other by a $15\text{ mm}$ fillet weld. Find the maximum torque that the welded joint can sustain if the permissible shear stress intensity in the weld material is not to exceed $80\text{ MPa}$.

---

### Example 10.4 (Parallel Fillet Weld — Static & Fatigue)
A plate $100\text{ mm}$ wide and $12.5\text{ mm}$ thick is to be welded to another plate by means of double parallel fillet welds. The plates are subjected to a load of $50\text{ kN}$. Find the length of the weld so that the maximum stress does not exceed $56\text{ MPa}$. Consider the joint first under static loading and then under fatigue loading ($K_f = 1.5$).

---

### Example 10.5 (Combination Transverse & Parallel Fillet Weld — Static & Fatigue)
A plate $75\text{ mm}$ wide and $12.5\text{ mm}$ thick is joined with another plate by a single transverse weld and a double parallel fillet weld. The maximum tensile and shear stresses are $70\text{ MPa}$ and $56\text{ MPa}$ respectively. Find the length of each parallel fillet weld for both static loading and fatigue loading ($K_{ft} = 1.5, K_{fp} = 2.7$).

---

### Example 10.6 (Weld Run Length under Variable Loading)
Determine the length of the weld run for a plate of size $120\text{ mm}$ wide and $15\text{ mm}$ thick to be welded to another plate by means of:
1. A single transverse weld; and
2. Double parallel fillet welds when the tensile load varies dynamically from $70\text{ kN}$ to $220\text{ kN}$.

---

### Example 10.7 (Fillet Weld 'T' Section Shear Plane Analysis)
The fillet welds of equal legs $s$ and length $l$ are used to fabricate a 'T' section. Locate the plane of maximum shear stress in:
1. Load parallel to the weld (neglect eccentricity); and
2. Load at right angles to the weld (transverse load).
Find the ratio of these limiting loads.

---

### Example 10.8 (Axially Loaded Unsymmetrical Section Angle Welding)
A $200 \times 150 \times 10\text{ mm}$ angle is to be welded to a steel plate by fillet welds. If the angle is subjected to a static load of $200\text{ kN}$, find the length of weld at the top and bottom. The allowable shear stress for static loading may be taken as $75\text{ MPa}$ and weld size $s = 10\text{ mm}$. Distance of C.G. from top edge $y_1 = 70.3\text{ mm}$, bottom edge $y_2 = 129.7\text{ mm}$.

---

### Example 10.9 (Eccentrically Loaded In-Plane Welded Joint)
A welded joint is subjected to an eccentric load $P = 2\text{ kN}$ at $e = 100\text{ mm}$. Find the size of weld $s$ if the maximum allowable shear stress in the weld is $25\text{ MPa}$.

---

### Example 10.10 (Circular Fillet Weld under Combined Bending and Torsion)
A $50\text{ mm}$ diameter solid shaft is welded to a flat plate by a $15\text{ mm}$ fillet weld. The shaft carries a bending load of $10\text{ kN}$ at a distance of $200\text{ mm}$ from the plate and transmits a torque of $1\text{ kN}\cdot\text{m}$. Find the maximum normal stress and maximum shear stress induced in the weld.

---

### Example 10.11 (Rectangular Cross-Section Bar Welded to Support under Bending)
A rectangular cross-section bar ($b = 100\text{ mm}, h = 150\text{ mm}$) is welded to a vertical support by means of fillet welds on all four sides. Determine the required size of the welds if an eccentric load $P = 25\text{ kN}$ acts at $e = 200\text{ mm}$ from the support and permissible shear stress in the weld is limited to $75\text{ MPa}$.

---

### Example 10.12 (Arm Welded to Hollow Shaft under Bending)
An arm A is welded to a hollow shaft at section '1'. The hollow shaft is welded to a plate C at section '2'. A force $P = 15\text{ kN}$ acts at arm A perpendicular to the axis of the arm. Calculate the size of weld required at section '1' and section '2' if allowable shear stress in the weld is $120\text{ MPa}$.

---

### Example 10.13 (Eccentrically Loaded Out-of-Plane Bracket Weld)
A bracket carrying a load of $15\text{ kN}$ is to be welded to a vertical column. Find the size of weld required if the allowable shear stress is not to exceed $80\text{ MPa}$.

---

### Example 10.14 (Cantilever Plate Weld under Bending)
A rectangular steel plate is welded as a cantilever to a vertical column and supports a single concentrated load $P = 50\text{ kN}$ at eccentricity $e = 150\text{ mm}$. Determine the weld size if shear stress in the weld is not to exceed $140\text{ MPa}$.

---

### Example 10.15 (Channel Section Welded to Plate under Eccentric Bending)
Find the maximum shear stress induced in the weld of $6\text{ mm}$ size when a channel section is welded to a plate and loaded with $20\text{ kN}$ force at an eccentric distance of $200\text{ mm}$.

---

### Example 10.16 (Shielded Arc Welded Bracket under Dead Weight)
The bracket carrying a dead weight of $P = 15\text{ kN}$ is designed with fillet welds at the top and bottom. Determine the required sizes of fillet welds if the permissible strength of the weld is $150\text{ MPa}$.
